## Key Points

- RRSI improves agent harnesses across all out-of-distribution benchmarks without overfitting to the specific dataset used during training
- Unlike previous methods that show gains only on their training data, RRSI maintains or improves performance when benchmark conditions change
- The approach focuses on regularizing the search process rather than modifying the harness components themselves
- RRSI applies constraints on how much a proposal can change and which measured gains are allowed to persist
- The method includes a proposal and selection system where each candidate is logged with its hypothesis, changes, score and cost improvements
- When progress stalls within noise margins, budget is redirected to untouched components of the system
- The system automatically rejects task names, entities, answers, or benchmark-specific logic before scoring candidates
- Every held-out benchmark shows improvement after training on one suite per domain
- Two specific rules govern costs: one refuses growth that isn't paid for when proposed, and another removes growth that stops paying for itself
- Research demonstrates consistent gains across multiple benchmarks including Harvey LAB (89.2), JobBench (40.7), GDPval (52.3), and APEX-Agents (37.9)
