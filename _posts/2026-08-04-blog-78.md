## Key Points

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei is reportedly worried that new hires join for money rather than mission, despite Anthropic paying the highest salaries in AI
Top AI researchers are constantly moving between labs, with examples including Lilian Weng leaving Thinking Machines for OpenAI and Google losing Noam Shazeer to OpenAI
The AI industry operates like a connected ecosystem with the same few hundred elite researchers circulating through different companies
Money alone isn't enough to retain talent - researchers also chase compute resources, influence over projects, freedom to work their own way, and equity before IPOs
When every lab can pay millions, mission becomes the only differentiating factor, but it's hard to verify genuine conviction amidst huge paychecks
Anthropic reportedly uses culture interviews that probe whether candidates 'fear the technology enough', which some critics mock as cult-like
More than 1,300 lab employees recently signed a letter warning that AI development could outrun control
Constant departures disrupt research continuity, force companies to pay repeatedly for the same talent pool, and lead to legal issues like Apple suing OpenAI over trade secrets
Companies can buy a researcher's time, but lasting loyalty isn't for sale - a lesson labs paying the most are learning first
The irony is that the companies insisting money isn't the main factor are the ones struggling with talent who might actually be motivated primarily by pay
