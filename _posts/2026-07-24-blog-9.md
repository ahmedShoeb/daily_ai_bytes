## Key Points

- The article critiques the practice of 'tokenmaxxing' - maximizing AI usage across development workflows under the assumption that heavy AI consumption automatically leads to better outcomes
- Organizations initially tracked AI usage via leaderboards, but these were easily gamed and didn't measure actual business value
- As AI costs increased, companies shifted to token minimization strategies, but this created similar problems by limiting the information AI systems need to succeed
- The article introduces 'valuemaxxing' - focusing on direct business outcomes rather than token counts, asking questions like 'How many bugs were prevented?' and 'How much faster was the release?'
- Competitive advantage is shifting from the AI models themselves to the systems built around them: context management, workflow orchestration, memory, and optimization
- Model orchestration is becoming more important than model selection, with IDC predicting 70% of leading AI-driven enterprises will use dynamic routing across multiple models by 2028
- AI efficiency is becoming a crucial engineering skill, similar to managing cloud resources or database performance
- Leaders need to create visibility into both costs and outcomes, measuring value instead of volume
- IBM Bob is presented as an example of a platform designed for valuemaxxing, with intelligent model routing, usage analytics, and transparency tools
- Regardless of future AI cost trends, organizations should focus on whether their AI investments create meaningful business value
