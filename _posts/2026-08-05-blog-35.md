## Key Points

. Microsoft appears to be preparing its first native real-time voice model called MAI Realtime, which is currently accessible to a small group of partners in the company's MAI Playground.
. The system is bidirectional and full-duplex, meaning it can listen and speak simultaneously instead of taking turns, putting it in direct competition with OpenAI's GPT Live 1 or Sesame.
. Two voices are currently available - Victoria and Grant - both noted to be more natural sounding than what Copilot's current voice mode delivers.
. The model can switch languages mid-conversation without losing context, with language settings that can be pinned explicitly or left on automatic detection.
. The system offers configurable turn-taking through two listener options: Switchboard mode using MAI-Ears endpointer, and a deterministic setup with silence-based and Whisper semantic endpointing.
. The model handles interruptions cleanly with low response latency, but it doesn't sing or produce non-speech sounds, keeping it focused on conversation rather than general audio generation.
. This would fill a gap in Microsoft's current offerings - all existing MAI speech models run in only one direction (either synthesis or recognition), while Azure Speech's Voice Live API still relies on OpenAI's GPT-Realtime model.
. A first-party full-duplex model would help Microsoft reduce dependency on OpenAI components as they've been systematically replacing them in Copilot, Teams, and Bing.
. Microsoft Foundry is likely the development destination, with Copilot voice being the obvious consumer-facing application, though no timeline has been announced.
