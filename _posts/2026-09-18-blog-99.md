## Key Points

- The author implemented an x402 protocol on their website that charges AI agents $0.01 per page access, with successful test payments made on September 15
- Cloudflare is developing similar wallet technology (Monetization Gateway) for pay-per-crawl models, though it's currently in closed beta and not yet publicly available
- The author successfully watched Claude Code pay for content using a hook during a task, with automated payment processing and budget limits in place
- Google's alternative AI contribution pilot pays publishers when their content contributes to AI answers, but offers limited transparency on payment calculations
- The demo uses testnet USDC tokens, with real money transactions planned as the next step when agents support the payment process
- Pay-per-crawl allows website owners to independently verify payments via blockchain receipts, unlike Google's black-box revenue sharing model
- Smaller publishers often block AI crawlers due to traffic imbalance, while larger publishers can negotiate licensing deals with AI companies
- The author emphasizes the importance of setting spending limits for AI agents to prevent them from emptying wallets without constraint
- While payment infrastructure exists, major search crawlers like GPTBot and Googlebot don't yet support wallet-based payment systems
- The author suggests charging only for specialized content like original datasets and research, while keeping general content free for discoverability
