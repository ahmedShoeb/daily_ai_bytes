## Key Points

- Prime Minister Mark Carney announced the Productivity Mega-Deduction on September 15, 2026, allowing businesses to write off 100% of eligible new investments (including computer equipment, software, R&D, and patents) in the first year
- The enhanced SR&ED program now provides a 35% refundable tax credit on up to $6 million of qualifying R&D spending per year, with capital equipment used at least 90% for eligible R&D also becoming claimable
- The Regional Artificial Intelligence Initiative (RAII) offers $200 million in interest-free, unsecured financing covering up to 50% of AI project costs ($250K-$5M) with a five-year repayment term starting one year after project completion
- These three programs interlock rather than overlap: mega-deduction covers capital costs, SR&ED covers R&D labor, and RAII provides financing
- For a $1 million AI stack example, businesses could receive about $231,500 back in year one through tax savings and refunds plus $500,000 interest-free financing
- Canada's marginal effective tax rate on new business investment will drop from roughly 13% to 6.4%, making it the lowest among major economies
- Proper sequencing is crucial: firms should engage with regional development agencies before incurring costs and maintain SR&ED documentation from day one
- The programs don't double-count costs, which is essential for compliance and maximizing benefits
- While the incentives are substantial, they come with important caveats including eligibility requirements, repayment obligations for RAII funding, and dependence on having taxable income
- These incentives are designed to significantly reduce the real cost of building private AI infrastructure for Canadian businesses
