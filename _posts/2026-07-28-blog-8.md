## Key Points

- Neura Robotics, a German startup founded in 2019, raised $55 million in new funding to expand its cognitive robotics business
- The company builds 'cobots' - cognitive robots with memory, ability to operate in complex environments, and collaborate with humans
- The funding comes from Lingotto (part of Exor N.V.), Vsquared Ventures, Primepulse and HV Capital, replacing previous strategic investor Han's Group
- Neura currently has three models: MAV mobile robot, LARA 'high-end cobot', and MAiRA (the 'world's first cognitive robot')
- The company has $450 million in orders over the next five years, but is limited by production capacity
- Prices range from €5,000 to €40,000 for industrial models, with upcoming MiPA service robot priced below €10,000
- Neura operates on a B2B/B2B2C model, with partners like Kawasaki already selling cobots based on their platform
- Founder David Reger describes cognitive robots as 'smartphones with arms and legs' - assistants that relieve humans physically
- The company believes in building both hardware and software/AI to create a complete robotic platform
- Neura plans to expand into consumer markets with MiPA service robots for offices, care facilities, and homes within 2 years
- The company has an open platform approach with APIs and app ecosystem called 'the Neuraverse'
