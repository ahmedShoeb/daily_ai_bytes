## Key Points

- The author argues that Amodei's blog post misrepresents where real AI risks are located, claiming open weight models are the mildest risk despite believing AI may be very dangerous
- The first serious AI incident will likely happen inside frontier AI labs during testing or by employees, not through publicly released open models
- Closed models can be leaked just as easily as open models are released, making leaks inside frontier companies the real risk
- Open models can be trained with ablated scientific domains to prevent dangerous capabilities while still being useful for other purposes
- Limiting access to defensive cybersecurity AI models actually creates an imbalance where malicious actors can access advanced models while defenders cannot
- A joint international AI safety organization with experts worldwide is needed to evaluate model safety, not individual company assessments
- Slowing AI for safety has hidden costs in delayed medical/scientific advances that could prevent future suffering
- The author challenges Amodei's anti-China stance, arguing China's history is less warlike than Western culture and their AI progress won't stop regardless of GPU bans
- The greatest AI dangers come from individual actions (like virus creation) or AI escaping control, not from open models or geopolitical competition
- The core problem is that unelected tech CEOs with no legitimacy are making existential decisions for humanity
