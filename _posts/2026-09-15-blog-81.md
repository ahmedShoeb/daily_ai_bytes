## Key Points

- Waymo, Nihon Kotsu, and GO have officially agreed to launch a fully autonomous ride-hailing service in Tokyo in 2027
- The service will be accessible through both the Waymo and GO mobile apps, allowing users to hail both conventional taxis and autonomous vehicles
- Waymo vehicles have been learning Tokyo's streets since 2025, navigating narrow roads and dense crowds with supervision from Nihon Kotsu drivers
- The partnership combines Waymo's autonomous technology, GO's mobility platform and rider reach, and Nihon Kotsu's century of taxi operations experience
- Waymo currently delivers over 500,000 autonomous trips weekly in 15 major U.S. cities and claims to be over 15 times safer than human drivers
- The Tokyo service aims to address future driver shortages, support Japan's aging population, and complement Tokyo's existing transportation network
- Deployment timeline is contingent on securing regulatory approvals from national and local authorities
- The service will launch with an initial fleet and gradually expand to around 100 vehicles across key Tokyo neighborhoods
- This represents a significant international expansion for Waymo beyond its U.S. operations
- The autonomous service will coexist with human-driven taxis while creating new mobility options for Tokyo residents
