## Key Points

- Meta acknowledges that its AI assistant Muse was 'definitely heavily inspired as a product by OpenClaw,' an open-source project
- Nat Friedman, head of product at Meta's Superintelligence Labs, clarified that Muse was 'built from scratch' despite the inspiration
- Early adopters had speculated Muse was OpenClaw repackaged, pointing to identical system files and nearly identical SOUL.md configuration files
- Friedman admitted Meta team 'thought that Peter [Steinberger, OpenClaw's creator] got those things exactly right,' explaining why file names and content were copied
- The comparison gained traction when an AI app co-founder claimed 'Muse is LITERALLY OpenClaw for normies' in a viral post
- Meta's approach follows its playbook of taking promising products and copying their best features, similar to its adoption of Snapchat's stories format
- Muse has been successful, recently hitting No. 1 on the U.S. App Store and outpacing ChatGPT's launch performance
- OpenClaw creator Peter Steinberger was described as a 'genius' whose work was 'pioneering from the jump' by Friedman
