## Key Points

(The article is an Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's statement clarifying the company's position on open-weights AI models.
Anthropic states it has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models despite recent accusations and industry debates.
Amodei identifies two primary security concerns: 1) Authoritarian governments building more powerful AI than the US, and 2) Misuse of AI for cyber/biological attacks.
Open-weights models without dangerous capabilities are considered a public good that provides value to businesses, developers, and researchers.
Protectionist bans on Chinese open-weights models would not address national security concerns because the most dangerous models would likely be kept secret.
Anthropic supports three key measures: restricting powerful chip sales to China, cracking down on industrial-scale distillation operations, and mandatory safety testing for all sufficiently capable models.
Distillation operations backed by authoritarian states are a particular concern as they allow China to build better models than its chip supply would normally enable.
Safety testing should apply to all capable models regardless of country of origin or whether they're open/closed, with less capable models exempted.
Amodei agrees with many benefits of open weights mentioned in recent industry letters but disagrees that they necessarily make safeguards easier to develop.
The company is committed to cracking down on distillation through its own practices while calling for broader policy solutions to address the challenge.
