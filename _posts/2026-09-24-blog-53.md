## Key Points

- MiMo-V2.6 represents one of the largest single RL runs by compute that any open-source team has undertaken, costing $2.6M for the larger model and $0.9M for the smaller one in RL post-training alone
- The system processes 1,568 prompts per training step with 16 attempts per prompt, accumulating 2.7-3.7B training tokens per step across coding, professional workflows, visual design, and cybersecurity domains
- Introduces Groupwise Advantage Redistribution (GAR) which grades multiple attempts simultaneously rather than using binary pass/fail feedback, ranking passing solutions by quality across five dimensions
- Without GAR, models learn to write longer, defensive patches that pass tests while accumulating bad habits; with GAR, improvements continue longer and models stay closer to requested scope
- Discovered that freezing the Mixture-of-Experts router is essential for stability during large-scale RL training - allowing router updates caused severe imbalance with a few experts absorbing all traffic
- On DeepSWE v1.1 benchmark, MiMo-V2.6-Pro's average@3 score improved from 58.4 to 72.6, showing significant performance gains from the scaled RL approach
- The team released MiMo-V2.6-Distill-Qwen-9B as a smaller model fine-tuned on MiMo demonstrations, along with 7K diverse environments and a complete RL training framework
- The approach demonstrates that RL scaling for agents isn't just about generating more rollouts, but building a larger, more varied, and better supervised learning loop
- The authors argue this addresses a fundamental limit of binary reward signals for agents working on complex, long-horizon tasks where multiple solutions can technically pass
- The research innovations and engineering challenges behind MiMo-V2.6 reportedly surpass those of DeepSeek R1 according to the authors
