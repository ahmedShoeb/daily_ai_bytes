## Key Points

- Aidan Gomez, CEO of Cohere, argues that a handful of Silicon Valley AI companies should not be allowed to define AI safety rules and standards for the entire world
- The article criticizes Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's proposal for antitrust exemptions that would allow dominant AI labs to coordinate on safety standards and development pace
- Historical examples show how similar approaches led to cartel-like structures in bond rating and automotive industries that ultimately failed to improve safety
- The proposal would let market-dominant companies set standards, determine technology advancement speed, and require all other developers to follow their rules
- Current safety frameworks focus on model scale, favoring companies with massive computational resources while ignoring risks from smaller, well-orchestrated systems
- Gomez proposes four pillars for better AI regulation: evidence-based risk frameworks, mandatory transparency, scoped testing based on evidence, and truly independent assurance mechanisms
- The article warns against focusing on hypothetical superintelligence risks while ignoring current harms like voice cloning fraud and automated decision-making biases
- Security requires technological diversity, local deployment, and sovereignty - not dependence on foreign monopolies
- The rules for this generational technology should be developed through open, democratic processes with diverse voices, not behind closed doors by commercially-aligned companies
- Cohere's approach emphasizes building AI systems that can run inside organizations' own infrastructure with providers who can be held accountable
