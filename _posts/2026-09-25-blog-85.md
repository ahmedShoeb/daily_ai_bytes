## Key Points

- China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) announced mass production of its fifth-generation DRAM technology platform
- The company claims its process capability is now on par with the most advanced mass-produced nodes in the industry
- The new platform yields at least 50% more dies per wafer than its fourth generation
- CXMT has released 24-gigabit LPDDR5X chips in two packages aimed at mid-range and high-end smartphones
- The company developed the platform using computer simulation and joint work with Chinese chip-equipment makers to bypass US export controls
- Four-exposure patterning technology allows CXMT to work around restrictions on advanced chipmaking equipment
- CXMT's DRAM has already appeared in Western products like Corsair's DDR5 kits (using fourth-gen platform)
- Acer's CEO expects PC prices to rise 5-20% in Q4 2022, with prices peaking mid-2027 due to memory shortages
- Key unknowns remain: actual yield rates, pricing competitiveness, and which Western manufacturers will adopt the chips at scale
- CXMT presents itself as a resilient supply chain alternative to the three major DRAM suppliers outside China
