## Key Points

- Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory's Advanced Light Source produces enormous amounts of X-ray imaging data - tens of petabytes annually - but manual analysis can't keep pace with modern detectors capturing 100,000 images per second
- The White House's Genesis Mission launched SYNAPS-I, a multi-lab initiative using AI to transform scientific data analysis from a months-long bottleneck into real-time discovery
- SYNAPS-I uses Meta's open-source Segment Anything Model 3 (SAM 3) and DINOv3 to automatically segment and analyze scientific images, reducing analysis time from weeks/months to just 15 minutes
- DINOv3 identifies structures in images while SAM draws precise boundaries, creating a pipeline that reconstructs 3D volumes with semantic labeling in near real-time
- The system was demonstrated on grapevine stem micro-CT scans, automatically identifying xylem vessels to study drought response at cellular level for agricultural resilience
- Open-source approach allows national labs to download, fine-tune, and deploy models within secure government infrastructure rather than using external cloud services
- The system runs on 300 A100 GPUs at national supercomputing facilities like NERSC, enabling real-time analysis while experiments are still running
- SYNAPS-I aims to create intelligent discovery platforms where AI helps generate hypotheses, recommends next experiments, and transfers knowledge across facilities
- DOE Under Secretary Dario Gil highlighted that this approach compresses discovery time from days to moments and establishes self-improving science models
- The project involves 60 researchers across five national labs working toward automated, adaptive scientific discovery systems
