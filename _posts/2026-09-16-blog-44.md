## Key Points

- Dwarkesh Patel interviews three AI researchers from 'open-ish' labs: Beren Millidge (CTO of Zyphra), John Schulman (chief scientist at Thinking Machines, ex-OpenAI), and Charlie O'Neill (head of model training at Baseten)
- The central question explores why we might NOT see billions of superintelligences radically transforming the world by 2036, examining technical bottlenecks beyond political regulations or wars
- Researchers discuss whether current AI paradigms (transformer + RL) can achieve recursive self-improvement (RSI) or if we need a fundamental architecture breakthrough
- Distillation emerges as a key factor preventing industry consolidation - even frontier models can be copied using techniques like extracting prompt distributions from router services
- Chinese labs' progress is attributed partly to accessing US frontier models through router services that collect and sell prompt data for distillation
- The debate examines whether 'taste' and long-horizon judgment can be learned through meta-learning or requires fundamentally different approaches
- Researchers discuss the sim-to-real gap and whether AI can learn everything needed for RSI from simulated environments rather than real-world deployment
- Rapid-fire timeline predictions: Most give 2-4 years for AI to become drop-in remote workers for white-collar work, but differ on timelines for AI dominating all cognitive work (3The transcript features extensive discussion of reinforcement learning, data vs. architecture scaling, continual learning challenges, and the sample efficiency gap between humans and AI models
