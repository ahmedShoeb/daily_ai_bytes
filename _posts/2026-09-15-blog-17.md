## Key Points

- Valve's Steam Frame is a VR headset that evolved from SteamOS hardware journey, capable of streaming PC titles or playing games standalone
- Despite component price increases, Valve kept the original configuration unchanged rather than cutting specs to hit lower price points
- The $1,000+ price tag reflects current component economics, similar to Steam Machine pricing challenges
- Half-Life: Alyx served as both a halo title and stress test for the rendering/driver stack, driving native ARM development without translation layers
- Foveated rendering is a major technical pillar, with Valve investing in Unity/Unreal support and creating a render pass optimizer for existing games
- Users can choose between streaming (2+ hours battery) or standalone (1-2 hours depending on game demands) play modes
- Experimental 144Hz mode focuses on smoother head tracking and world rendering, not necessarily native 144fps gameplay
- Steam Frame uses monochrome cameras for better low-light tracking instead of RGB cameras, prioritizing gaming over AR applications
- Valve sees Steam Frame as more than just a headset - it's essentially a PC-on-your-head running SteamOS with desktop-like capabilities
- The modular design allows for third-party accessories and DIY experimentation, similar to Steam Deck's open ecosystem approach
