## Key Points

AWS announced it cannot restore access to cloud-computing facilities in Bahrain and parts of the UAE six months after drone strikes damaged the sites during the early days of the Iran war
The company stated that damage at multiple Bahrain-based sites exceeded what its services are designed to withstand
AWS has determined it's unable to restore access to resources and data hosted exclusively in the Bahrain region
Similarly, AWS cannot recover resources and data stored in one of its availability zones in the UAE, while continuing efforts to restore two other affected UAE zones
This is AWS' first public update on the incidents since April, following March attacks where UAE data centers were directly struck and a Bahrain facility damaged
Amazon had previously advised customers to move workloads to other AWS regions due to continued instability in the Middle East
Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed responsibility for targeting the Bahrain facility over Amazon's support of the U.S. military
The attacks have raised security concerns about future data center developments in the Gulf region, which was capitalizing on AI infrastructure demand
The UAE is considering revising its massive 'Stargate' project to disperse facilities across the country with enhanced security measures
Amazon remains committed to supporting customers in Bahrain and UAE, with further updates expected in the coming months for UAE and early 2027 for Bahrain
