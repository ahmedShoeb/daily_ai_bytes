## Key Points

- Dario Amodei (CEO of Anthropic) called for the AI industry to pace itself, but the definition of 'pacing' remains unclear
- Five major perspectives exist on AI pacing: interpretability (wanting time for safety research), labor (protecting workers), economic (seeing AI as key to managing national debt), geopolitical (maintaining strategic advantage), and regulatory capture (avoiding new rules)
- The interpretability camp, led by Anthropic's Evan Hubinger, believes there's over 10% chance AI could go catastrophically wrong for humanity within a decade without better understanding of models
- The economic camp sees AI investment as disinflationary and productive, with predictions that benefits will appear within six months
- The geopolitical camp focuses on maintaining U.S. leadership over China in the AI race
- Pacing proposals involve limiting compute and training runs for frontier models, though compute thresholds have historically been ineffective due to rapid technological advancement
- Frontier AI models now maintain leads for months (up from days in early 2025), and the gap between open and closed models has widened from three to four months
- Training compute grows about fivefold per year, making fixed thresholds quickly obsolete
- The fundamental questions are not just how fast AI should develop, but who gets to decide and what precise intervals should be enforced
