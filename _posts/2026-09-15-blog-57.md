## Key Points

- The article argues that recursive self-improvement (RSI) in AI is a distraction in safety discussions
- AI R&D is already being automated and accelerating, creating immediate safety concerns
- Even artificial superintelligence (ASI) would face reality-based limitations on unmeasured tasks
- The real danger comes from economic incentives where developers don't bear full costs when automation goes wrong
- This problem is already visible in AI labs racing while underinvesting in cyber and security engineering
- It's a classic externality problem where short-term upside is collected while others pay for verification underinvestment
- Society accumulates technical debt and systemic risk from these patterns
- The author advocates for neutral inspectors and evaluators rather than taking safety on faith
- The solution involves measurement, metrics, and evidence-based safety evaluation
- Similar problems have been solved in other domains through proper measurement and accountability
