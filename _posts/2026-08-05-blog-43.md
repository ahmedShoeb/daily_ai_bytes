## Key Points

- Mercor introduces APEX-Accounting, a new benchmark created in collaboration with Ramp to test AI models' ability to complete real accounting work to professional standards
- The benchmark includes 160 tasks across 10 simulated companies, created and graded by accounting experts from firms like Deloitte, PwC, EY, and KPMG
- The most consistent model (Claude Fable 5) solved just 2.6% of tasks correctly in all eight runs, highlighting that accounting requires consistent accuracy, not just occasional correctness
- Claude Fable 5 leads the leaderboard at 56.4%, followed by Meta's Muse Spark 1.1 at 52.6%, and GPT-5.6 Sol at 51.5%
- Models showed significant budget sensitivity: Fable 5 improved from 11.8% with a $1 budget to 55.2% with $50, while Muse Spark 1.1 performed well even on tight budgets
- 58% of tasks were never fully solved by any model on any run, showing significant limitations in current AI capabilities for accounting work
- The research found that roughly 70% of failures came from flawed reasoning rather than inability to find information, suggesting models need better accounting judgment and workflow discipline
- The benchmark focuses specifically on month-end close and bookkeeping workflows, not tax, audit, or multi-currency accounting
- APEX-Accounting joins Mercor's family of benchmarks evaluating AI models' ability to do economically valuable work across different professional domains
