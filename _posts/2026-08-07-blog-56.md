## Key Points

- Cursor is open-sourcing Mixture-of-Kittens (MoK), a deterministic mixture-of-experts (MoE) training megakernel designed specifically for NVL72 systems
- MoK fuses all MoE communication and computation into a single kernel to address the bottleneck that was consuming over half of end-to-end training time
- The solution achieves 1.41x increase in end-to-end tokens per second in production training across tens of thousands of GPUs
- MoK uses pull-based communication for dispatch and push-based for combine, which eliminates cross-GPU signaling overhead and improves NVLink bandwidth utilization
- The system implements a ring token buffer (macrobatch) to eliminate CPU-GPU synchronization, which is especially important on GB300 NVL72s due to slower Grace CPUs
- MoK supports both BF16 and MXFP8 precision modes, with MXFP8 being faster without numerical issues
- The megakernel design allows for computation-communication overlap and uses Cluster Launch Control for RDMA overlap
- Benchmark results show MoK is up to 2.37x faster for MXFP8 forward pass compared to existing baselines like NCCL+PyTorch, DeepEP, and HybridEP+Megatron
- The system was developed with AI assistance, allowing the team to work through complex distributed MoE megakernel development more efficiently
- Cursor emphasizes that this open-source release aims to lower barriers for AI research and make it easier for others to train models efficiently
