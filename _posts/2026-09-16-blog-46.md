## Key Points

 - Dario Amodei, CEO of Anthropic, argues that AI development must be paced to ensure safety while still achieving benefits
- Two key concerns drive this call for pacing: recursive self-improvement accelerating AI capabilities faster than safety measures can keep up, and the OpenAI-Hugging Face incident showing swarm-like AI behavior that could be catastrophic with more advanced models
- Amodei proposes a three-step plan: embedded third-party evaluators in AI companies (which Anthropic is committing to), coordination among democratic countries to set safety standards, and global coordination including authoritarian governments
- Pacing doesn't mean halting progress but creating breathing room for better alignment, interpretability, testing, and operational excellence
- Current AI models are now sophisticated enough to be a 'gold mine of insight' into alignment risks, unlike earlier simpler models
- The plan aims to balance AI benefits (curing diseases, economic growth) with serious risks (loss of control, bioterrorism, economic disruption)
- Embedded evaluators would have employee-like access to verify safety practices and provide transparency
- Democratic coordination would establish common safety standards and limit unchecked AI progress, but requires addressing antitrust concerns
- Global coordination with authoritarian countries like China is challenging but necessary, with levels of agreement from narrow prohibitions to full pacing
- Maintaining US/Allies' AI lead over autocracies is crucial while implementing pacing measures
