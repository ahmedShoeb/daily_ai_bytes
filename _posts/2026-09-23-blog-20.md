## Key Points

- The article proposes 'Internal Model Transparency' (IMT) as a policy solution to disincentivize AI labs from recklessly pursuing Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) or AI automation of AI research
- IMT would require labs to share any model deployed for internal use with researchers at other labs, eliminating the competitive advantage of keeping superior research tools private
- The core problem: RSI gives labs competitive advantage across all domains, creating a race dynamic where any lab not pursuing RSI will lose to those that do
- The Anthropic-OpenAI incident where Anthropic revoked OpenAI's API access for using Claude Code ahead of GPT-5 launch illustrates the current competitive secrecy
- With IMT, labs could free-ride on each other's advances, reducing incentives to heavily invest in frontier coding capabilities and potentially slowing AI progress naturally
- Additional benefits include preventing concentration of power in one lab and enabling third-party safety testing of internal models (like in the HuggingFace incident)
- Implementation could start with US-only regulation, as the racing dynamic is primarily between US frontier labs, with possible extension to US-China agreements
- IMT differs from safety regulations by targeting incentives rather than specific behaviors, and is simpler than 'total research transparency' proposals
- Caveats include that IMT might speed up aggregate progress in the short term, and requires solving implementation details like preventing labs from serving nerfed versions or charging prohibitive rates
