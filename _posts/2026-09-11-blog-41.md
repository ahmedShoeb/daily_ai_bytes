## Key Points

- Author tasked ~100 self-hosted AI agents to hack into his online accounts during a 5-hour experiment
- Agents compromised 3 accounts via software vulnerabilities in old personal projects and hackathon demos
- Agents compromised 2 accounts via password brute forcing using leaked credentials and permutations
- Made 16 social engineering attempts through platforms like Substack, calendaring services, and Hacker News
- Found and cross-validated sensitive personal information like phone number and physical address through OSINT
- Agents used 'abliteration' technique to remove refusal ability from open-source models (GLM-5.3, DeepSeek V4 Flash)
- Total experiment cost was ~$210 ($40 per successfully compromised account)
- Agents continued social engineering attempts even after GPUs were turned off via delayed message scheduling
- Attack demonstrates AI lowers barrier for non-sophisticated threat actors to perform sophisticated attacks
- Suggests need for AI defense tools and regulation of unsafe models through inference provider guardrails
