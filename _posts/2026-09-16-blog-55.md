## Key Points

| The paper challenges the notion that frontier language models struggle with advanced physics, suggesting current benchmarks significantly understate their capabilities
- Researchers evaluated frontier models on six physics benchmarks with expert auditing to distinguish genuine model errors from benchmarking issues
- Most cases initially evaluated as incorrect were due to grader errors, incorrect reference solutions, or ambiguous questions rather than model reasoning errors
- After expert corrections (fixing reference solutions and removing flawed questions), GPT-5.6-Sol's performance dramatically improved: from 47.3% to 78.7% on HLE-Physics and from 61.0% to 87.2% on CMT-Benchmark
- On the retained CritPt challenges, GPT-5.6-Sol achieved 94.4% pass@4 after expert corrections
- Similar substantial improvements were seen across UGPhysics, PRISM-Physics, and PHYBench benchmarks after expert review and correction
- The findings suggest current physics benchmarks are 'broken' and need expert validation to accurately assess model capabilities
- Near-saturation of corrected scores indicates the need for more demanding, expert-validated evaluations beyond current benchmark tasks
