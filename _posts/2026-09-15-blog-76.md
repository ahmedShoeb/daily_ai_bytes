## Key Points

- A DeepSeek engineer wrote a candid reflection about building AI technology that may replace him within 6 months to a year
- He specifically works on attention kernels for AI models and acknowledges AI is rapidly approaching his level of expertise
- Despite knowing his work accelerates his own obsolescence, he continues because he loves the creative challenge of kernel optimization
- He recognizes that even if he slowed down, other companies would continue advancing AI development
- The engineer believes he won't become unemployed but will have to 'change professions' from direct kernel design to becoming an 'AI mecha pilot'
- He compares the situation to a master knitter whose craft is overtaken by a magical knitting machine - the work becomes faster but the personal joy is lost
- The essay expresses concern about broader societal impacts, including students using AI to bypass skill development and potential inequality in AI access
- He fears a future where advanced AI is controlled by a few companies, creating a Cyberpunk 2077-like dystopia rather than equitable distribution
- This concern is why he chose to work at DeepSeek - to develop open-source AI accessible to everyone
- The article represents a poignant personal struggle between technological progress and the loss of meaningful work
