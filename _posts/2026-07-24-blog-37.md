## Key Points

-full: Simulation has become essential for training physical AI systems because real-world data collection is slow, expensive, and sometimes destructive
- Developers use simulation to generate large amounts of photorealistic, physically grounded data through GPU parallelism
- Modern simulation is part of the model development loop for perception datasets, reinforcement learning, demonstrations, and testing
- The three-computer paradigm includes: training computers (GPU clusters), simulation computers (GPU workstations), and on-robot computers (edge devices)
- Popular simulation engines include NVIDIA Isaac Sim/Isaac Lab, MuJoCo/MuJoCo Warp, PyBullet, Drake, and Genesis
- MuJoCo focuses on precise dynamics and contact-rich motion rather than visual realism
- MuJoCo Warp is GPU-accelerated for large-scale reinforcement learning and policy training
- Isaac Sim uses OpenUSD and provides high-fidelity physics with photorealistic RTX rendering
- Isaac Lab 3.0 is a lightweight, GPU-accelerated framework for robot learning that decouples from Isaac Sim
- Newton is an open-source GPU-accelerated physics engine developed by NVIDIA, Google DeepMind, and Disney Research
- Simulation is now foundational for embodied AI, enabling stress-testing policies and amplifying data
- Much of the core infrastructure is now open-source, lowering barriers to entry for physical AI development
