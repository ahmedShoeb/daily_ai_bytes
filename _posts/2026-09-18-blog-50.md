## Key Points

- Standard benchmarks hide agent reliability issues - GPT-4.1 agent scoring 77.4% average success succeeds on all 5 runs for only 53.0% of tasks, creating a 24.4-point consistency gap
- The Consistency Analyzer diagnoses flip-prone decision points by resampling individual steps in recorded trajectories, requiring just one additional model call per decision
- Consistency guidelines automatically generated from these diagnoses cut the consistency gap roughly in half (24.4pp → 12.0pp) without sacrificing average accuracy
- The system identifies 'flat' vs 'sharp' probability distributions - when an agent's choices have near-tied probabilities, small platform variations can cause different outcomes
- The solution works across difficulty levels, with medium and hard tasks showing the most improvement (Medium +22.9pp, Hard +14.3pp)
- Guidelines generalize to similar tasks, achieving +13.0pp improvement on related tasks, showing they capture reusable patterns rather than just patching specific failures
- The approach doesn't require bigger models, as consistency is orthogonal to capability - stronger models improve average success but not necessarily reliability
- The method requires no ground truth or end-to-end task replay - just one extra model call per decision point, making it practical for production systems
- IBM Research emphasizes the importance of reporting Pass^k (all k runs must succeed) alongside traditional Mean@k metrics to properly assess agent reliability
