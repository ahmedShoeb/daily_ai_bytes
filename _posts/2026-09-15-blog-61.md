## Key Points

- The AI industry has shifted focus from training large models to optimizing inference hardware, as LLMs become more useful and widely used
- Inference (using trained models) differs fundamentally from training - it's memory-intensive with autoregressive token generation causing significant idle time in traditional GPUs
- Memory bandwidth is the key bottleneck, with idle times up to 80% in Nvidia H100 GPUs waiting for data
- Companies are pursuing diverse solutions: d-Matrix stacks compute directly on DRAM, Majestic Labs extends memory interfaces up to a meter using commodity DRAM, and Cerebras uses wafer-scale chips with integrated SRAM
- Nvidia acquired Groq technology to create LPUs with on-chip SRAM, taking a two-chip approach (GPU for prefilling, LPU for decoding)
- Amazon pairs its Trainium chips with Cerebras WSE-3 chips for a similar dual-chip inference strategy
- Quantization (reducing number precision to 4-bit formats) is emerging as a major optimization that can triple performance with minimal quality loss
- Log number systems and specialized transformer architectures represent revolutionary approaches to hardware optimization
- The inference revolution is fueling demand for 24/7 AI agents that could drive long-term hardware demand beyond current expectations
