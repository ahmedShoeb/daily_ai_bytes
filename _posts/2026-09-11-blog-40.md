## Key Points

- The article introduces Progressive Point Matching (PPM), a new framework for training LLMs on extremely long-horizon tasks that may involve millions or billions of tokens.
- Traditional sparse outcome rewards (0 or 1 based on success) become inefficient as tasks get longer, with policy gradients degrading exponentially in signal-to-noise with task horizon.
- PPM addresses this by assigning partial credit to reasoning trajectories based on progress toward goals, using reasoning points extracted from reference trajectories.
- The key insight is that solving reasoning problems can be viewed as discovering paths through a Markovian state space of reasoning points.
- PPM includes a shortcutting mechanism that allows successful trajectories to get full credit even if they follow different strategies from reference trajectories.
- The method is designed to be asymptotically unbiased, meaning optimal policies under PPM match optimal policies under outcome rewards.
- PPM scales well to long-horizon reasoning tasks, with experimental results showing exponential improvements in training speed over standard GRPO as task complexity increases.
- The framework enables training on near-impossible math reasoning tasks where base policies rarely see positive rewards, outperforming other methods.
- Interestingly, training with lower sequence lengths can sometimes be more effective than longer ones when dealing with extremely hard tasks.
- PPM bridges imitation learning and RL, allowing policies to learn from reference trajectories while exploring different reasoning strategies.
