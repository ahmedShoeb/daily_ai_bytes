## Key Points

- Sakana AI introduces PC-ALM (Augmented Lagrangian Predictive Coding), a layer-local alternative to backpropagation for training deep neural networks
- Traditional backpropagation requires strict phase locking (forward pass, backward pass, weight update) that the brain can't implement, while PC-ALM uses only local dynamics between neighboring layers
- PC-ALM successfully trains residual MLPs up to 1,000 layers, nearly matching backpropagation's performance despite using only local computation
- The method introduces Lagrange multipliers (dual variables) per layer, which accumulate prediction errors and can recover exact backprop credit signals in linear networks
- Compared to standard predictive coding (PC), PC-ALM overcomes the signal decay problem that limits PC's performance in deep, narrow networks
- PC-ALM exhibits 'ballistic' credit propagation where credit signals move through the network faster than standard PC's diffusive heat-flow-like propagation
- Individual neurons in PC-ALM show damped oscillations during inference, suggesting interesting dynamical properties
- The method has applications for neuromorphic hardware where dynamical systems simulation is more energy-efficient than GPU computation
- Primary motivation is understanding how distributed systems like brains can implement gradient computations without backpropagation
- Research bridges neuroscience (predictive coding theory) and distributed optimization (augmented Lagrangian methods) communities
