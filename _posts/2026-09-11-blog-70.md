## Key Points

- Bill Gates recently warned in a 6,000-word essay that AI could become history's greatest equalizer or worst source of injustice, predicting turbulent transitions
- The author argues Gates focuses too far in the future and overlooks current accountability issues with AI companies, investors, banks, and hyperscalers
- Current AI-related layoffs are driven by executives' decisions, not AI capabilities surpassing workers yet, as shown by Klarna's experience
- The author emphasizes that generating information with AI differs from reliable real-world action, requiring physical testing and engineering
- Systemic regulation should target AI companies and their financiers first, rather than hypothetical autonomous machines
- The article critiques Gates' proposal for multinational AI governance as potentially allowing tech billionaires to retain influence
- AI financing creates systemic risks as companies borrow billions against projections while ordinary people need documented income for small loans
- The article argues philanthropy cannot replace democratic accountability in AI governance
