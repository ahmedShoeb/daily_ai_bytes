## Key Points

The study examined AI progress from 2019-2025, finding that 3.24x more compute efficiency gains came from data improvements (12.0x) rather than model improvements (3.7x) at the 1e19 FLOPs compute budget
Researchers compared year-representative model recipes and data corpuses, evaluating models on end capabilities rather than cross-entropy loss
From 2019-2025, data improvements showed 1.51x year-over-year compute efficiency gains, while model improvements showed 1.24x gains
The gains from data and model improvements were mostly independent, with 88% of performance variance explained by additive effects rather than complex interactions
Data improvements evolved from OpenWebText (9B tokens) in 2019 to modern corpuses like UltraFineWeb that use sophisticated filtering and classification
Model improvements' main value wasn't necessarily compute efficiency, but making larger amounts of compute usable through innovations like MoEs, sparse attention, FlashAttention, and stability optimizations
The study suggests that with modern 'container ship' models having excess capacity, aggressive data curation may be less important than simply having larger datasets
Important unanswered questions include whether synthetic data can effectively expand data corpuses and whether we're approaching a 'data wall'
The researchers acknowledge limitations including small-scale experiments and focus on specific benchmarks, suggesting future research at larger scales
